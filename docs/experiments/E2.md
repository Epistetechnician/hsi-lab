# E2 — verified synthetic experience vs unverified vs real-only

Packet: `packets/e2-v1.frozen.json` (12 examples per arm; verified/unverified arms = 6 real seed
transitions + 6 synthetic; threshold +0.05 vs *both* baselines; family-regression floor 2 points).

Mechanism under test: a blind generator induces a model from the 6 real seed transitions and
proposes one-step trajectories from fresh start states with its own predicted outcome. Proposals go
to a quarantine queue. The verified arm re-executes each proposal against the environment oracle
and accepts only exact matches (rejecting outcome mismatches, duplicates, and provenance-free
items); the unverified arm trusts proposals as-is. Real-only gets 12 real random-walk transitions.

## Result: NO_CANDIDATE (receipt `E2-receipt.json`, independently recomputed)

| Comparison (verified vs …) | diff | 95% CI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unverified synthetic | +0.032 | [0.018, 0.046] |
| real-only | −0.189 | [−0.216, −0.163] |

Verification helps a little relative to trusting the generator (about 3 points), but neither beats
real data. Guardrail "no family regresses vs real-only beyond 2 points" fails (worst family
−0.22).

Why (mechanistic reading, not tested here): the oracle rejects ~73% of proposals (1279 of 1741
logged decisions were `outcome_mismatch`), and the proposals it accepts are precisely those where the
generator's hypothesis already agrees with the truth, so they carry little new information; the
informative (wrong) ones are discarded. Verification removes poison but cannot add knowledge the
generator lacked. A different generator (e.g. one that probes rather than extrapolates) is a new
packet, not a retry.

Nonclaims: scripted learner on a synthetic grid world; one generator design; says nothing about LLM
generators or real data.

Auditability: `run/` holds the quarantine ledger, decision log (digest-chained), examples ledger,
and rollouts; every verified example links to its real parents, a quarantine record, and an
`ok` decision (tested).
