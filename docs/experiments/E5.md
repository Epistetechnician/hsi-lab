# E5 — adaptive graph vs fixed graph under a byte cap

Packet: `packets/e5-v1.frozen.json`. Four agents, each able to execute only its own action
(agent k ↔ action k); the planner (agent 0) needs the others' learned rules. Identical exploration
(6 calls per agent) in both arms; one shared byte cap per task (72 B). Two arms:
- **fixed_graph** — static path 0–1–2–3, 3 rounds of delta-sync gossip (a chunk crosses each edge
  at most once), messages truncated to whole chunks that fit the cap.
- **adaptive_graph** — the planner opens a link to the owner of an action it lacks, receives that
  owner's rule, drops the link, and stops as soon as its model can plan. Links formed/dropped are
  logged (`run/decisions.jsonl`); churn is reported.

Primary: paired success within the cap (threshold +0.05). Guardrails: success per KB of adaptive ≥
fixed (recomputed by the validator from family counts and byte totals), cap respected, exploration
matched; link churn reported.

## Result: see `E5-receipt.json` (PROMOTE, independently recomputed and reproduced)

Adaptive − fixed = +0.421, 95% CI [0.385, 0.457] (n=400 per family; pilot flagged underpowered at
max_n). Success: rule_induction 0.655 vs 0.325, compositional_transfer 0.650 vs 0.138 (adaptive vs fixed);
success per KB is about 25.5 for adaptive vs 3.5 for fixed (receipt guardrail values); mean link
churn is 4.5 events per task (≈2.3 links formed and dropped).

## Read this before trusting it: the win is regime-dependent

Diagnostic sweep over the cap (tune-type seeds, 120 tasks per row; not part of the receipt):

| cap (B) | fixed success | adaptive success | fixed mean bytes | adaptive mean bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24 | 0.39 | 0.42 | 23 | 19 |
| 48 | 0.39 | 0.75 | 45 | 26 |
| 72 | 0.39 | 0.75 | 67 | 26 |
| 120 | 0.67 | 0.75 | 102 | 26 |
| 200 | 0.80 | 0.75 | 107 | 26 |

At the packet's cap (72 B) gossip spends its budget on irrelevant recipients before the far
agents' rules reach the planner, so adaptive wins by a wide margin. With a generous cap (≥200 B)
the fixed graph delivers everything and is slightly *better* on success (adaptive stops early on a
plan its model merely believes in) while still using ~4× the bytes; adaptive's lasting advantage is
efficiency (bytes), not accuracy. The cap in the packet was chosen so that it binds for gossip
before looking at results from the confirmatory cohort, but it is a design choice, not a neutral
one. Other fixed topologies (a ring, delta-sync with demand hints) are not tested.

Nonclaims: scripted agents; four agents; one cap; synthetic rule worlds.
