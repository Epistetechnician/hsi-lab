# E10 (packet e10-v2): does the real-data share of the training set matter?

Replication on PyTorch/CUDA (H100, one node, ~$10.5): full-precision Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, bf16 LoRA, 545 held-out
worlds (rule_induction + compositional_transfer, tune split), fresh lock created on the node. Ten chains to depth 2
(synthetic share 0, 11%, 25%, 50%, 75% of 350 examples; two replicates at 0 and 50%) plus a retrained `real_only`.
Source commit `96598de`, receipt `E10-receipt.json` (digest `33e693b565f5…`), recomputed OK by the stdlib validator
(A2). Design: ADR 0009. This is **not** an A3 reproduction: the MLX packet e10-v1 was abandoned locally (6 of 10
chains trained, RAM overload) and e10-v2 uses a different backend, so results are a replication, not a rerun.

## Result: INCONCLUSIVE
Primary d2_s050a minus d2_s000a: **+0.038, 95% CI [+0.014, +0.062]**, threshold +0.05. Positive and excludes zero,
but the bar is inside the interval.

## Reading
- The share effect does not replicate across chains: replicate b shows -0.003 for the same contrast. The
  two s000 chains differ by 0.021, and the primary effect (0.038) is under twice that.
- Non-monotonic: 11% and 25% show nothing, 50% and 75% show a small gain over s000a, 75% vs 50% is flat.
- s000a trails real_only by 0.033; s050a equals it. Consistent with the synthetic data adding little beyond
  the real data, and with s000a being a weak chain.
- Not supported: "a larger real-data share causes better chains" at the +0.05 bar. Not refuted either; the
  design cannot separate share from chain noise at this effect size.
- Diversity at the 50% and 75% shares fell (0.195 / 0.26 train-side), a possible cost; the receipt's guardrail
  flag was true.
